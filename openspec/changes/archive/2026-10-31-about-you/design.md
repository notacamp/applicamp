# Design

The `profile` module owns the data (schema `profile`). Pending state lives in two new tables of that schema; saved results live in the profile document (`profile.profiles.data`) and the goals row, so the existing `profile.updated` and `profile.goals_updated` events (ids and version only) are published by Save. Every endpoint needs a signed-in session (no scope: it is the person's own profile) and works only on that account's rows. Errors are problem+json.

## Data
- **Profile document**: `basics.background` (optional long text, new: domains and earlier careers in the person's words; written only by this wizard, never by the import or the structuring model, so it is `#[schemars(skip)]` like `facts`) and `work[].facts[]` gains `status` (`told`, the default and omitted, or `confirmed_assumption`) and `basis` (for a confirmed assumption: the reason it was assumed, or the page it was read on). A confirmed assumption's `quote` is the assumption as the person confirmed it. The limits and tidying treat them like other long values.
- `profile.about_you` (one row per account): `profile_version` the portrait was built for (null before), `portrait` (the judged sentences), `employers` (what researching each came to), `choices` (sentence id to `right | wrong | later`), `accuracy`, `extra` (their words, at most 4,000 characters), `changes` (pending changes with flags and resolutions), timestamps and `expires_at`. Pending state lasts 7 days from the last touch; the same sweep as imports and drafts (`profile.expire_imports`, enqueued at each expiry) deletes it. Save deletes it.
- `profile.about_calls` (id, account, kind `portrait | extra | research`, created_at): the wizard's call log, which the daily cap counts.

## Employer research is a port (no copy, no loop)
`profile::ports::EmployerResearch` (`known_site`, `set_site`, `run`) is declared by the profile module and implemented by the server over the pipeline, so modules never import each other (`architecture.toml` is unchanged). `run` returns `ProposedFact { claim, status: sourced | assumed, quote?, source_url?, reason }`; the port's contract is that **sourced needs a quote and its page**, and the profile downgrades any fact that claims `sourced` without both (`ProposedFact::checked`). The model's general knowledge is `assumed`, including what is typical for a role, and never reaches the portrait as a source.

The server's implementation reuses the pipeline's company facts, **one mechanism and one cache** for a past employer and a job's company (the company is the same `pipeline.companies` row, by name): `pipeline::features::brief::employer::{site_of, set_site, facts}`. The brief's `read_company` is split so its core, `fetch_and_store` (cap check, company on record, `Neighbours::read_site` for robots, host guard and same-site redirects, the facts call, the quote-on-page judge, storing facts and pages), serves both. A fresh company (read within 30 days) costs nothing. Page text is untrusted data: it is fenced inside the pipeline's own prompt and the portrait receives only the judged fact texts, as JSON data. **Only a known site is fetched**: the person typed it here or on a job; otherwise the employer is reported as `no_site` and nothing is fetched. A read that fails (robots disallows, unreachable, the daily company-read cap) is reported per employer and does not stop the portrait; a spending limit ends the step.

Departure from "assumed" research facts: the pipeline mechanism only produces sourced facts (an unsourced one is dropped by its judge), so employer research returns sourced facts only. What is assumed from knowing the role comes from the portrait call, marked `assumed` with a reason.

## The portrait call (`features/about/prompt.rs`, `judge.rs`)
`complete` role, temperature 0, strict JSON schema, answer budget 4000 tokens and one retry at 8000 after a cut-off. The system prompt is in code, versioned (`PROMPT_VERSION`, logged as a number). The user message holds the language, today's date, the positions (index, title, company, dates) and `<sources>`, a JSON list of `{id, kind, text}` built from the profile **without name, email, phone, address (location included) or links**, the kept facts (kind `told`), the goals, a computed line with the years since the first position (so "about 9 years" can be checked), and the researched employer facts (kind `research`, with their quote and page). All of it is data. A test asserts on the built messages that no contact detail is present.

Output: `{ sentences: [{text, kind known | told | assumed, sources, why, topic, position}] }`. The model never writes ids; the server numbers sentences `s1…`.

What code trusts (no model, no keywords):
- `known`: every claim in the sentence (numbers, technologies, capitalised names; `kernel::claims`) must appear in the texts of the profile or fact sources it cites. `told`: the same, and it may cite only facts the person wrote or confirmed. A sentence that fails, or cites nothing usable, is **downgraded to `assumed`** with `downgraded: true` (the client says "This isn't in your profile in these words."). Never dropped.
- `assumed`: not checked; its reason is kept; its `position` must exist. Research sources it cites show their page. The person decides.
- At most 16 sentences of 600 characters. Choices belong to the portrait they were made on.

## The "Anything else" call
One call, same role and budget. Input: the language, what the profile holds now (headline, summary, background, skill names, goals, positions by index; no contact details) and the text between tags. Output: `{ changes: [{field, value, position, area, quote, sentence}] }` with `field` one of `summary | headline | skill | background | goal_role | goal_avoid | goal_must_have | goal_location | role_fact`. Code (`judge_changes`):
- The **quote** must be a verbatim part of their words (`squash`); else a `quote` flag.
- Every claim in the **value** must appear in their words; each that does not is a `claim` flag naming it.
- Flagged changes are **kept and shown**. The portrait's *told* sentence is the model's sentence only if their words support it, else their quote, else the value.
- At most 12 changes, one summary, one headline, one background, no repeats, values cut to their field's limit. A skill is matched to existing ones and aliases at Save with `kernel::tech::same_skill` (so "Postgres" is not a second "PostgreSQL").
- Settling a flag: *It's true* marks it settled; *Fix* (and Edit) sets a new value that is checked again against their words, with flags again if it still says something they did not; *Use my own words* puts their exact words in the field (for the summary the whole answer, trimmed). Remove takes the change out.
- A new submission replaces the changes the last one made; submitting the same text again makes no call.

## Review and Save
`GET /about/review` groups pending changes and confirmed assumptions by part of the profile and builds the final portrait (profile and told sentences, the confirmed assumptions, the new told ones; unconfirmed assumptions are left out and counted). **Save** (`POST /about/save`) holds nothing back but a flag still waiting (409 `changes_flagged`; flagged lines are never silently dropped). In one transaction, with the profile and goals rows locked: apply every change and every confirmed assumption to the current profile (a hand edit in between is kept; a position is found by its key, company, title and start, not its place), write the profile once (source `answer`; the version trigger adds one), write the goals once if they changed (version plus one, `profile.goals_updated`), and delete the pending state. Items that no longer fit (a position removed, a goals list full) are counted as `skipped`, not an error. Facts already on the position with the same text are not duplicated. The gap review is not carried forward, so #24's strength and review refresh from the new version.

## Endpoints (`/api/v1/profile/about…`, session, OpenAPI)
`GET /about` (state; never calls the model; employers as they are now) · `POST /about/portrait?locale&again` (1 call per profile version plus one per employer read; the cached portrait with none) · `PUT /about/sentences/{id} {choice}` · `PUT /about/accuracy` · `PUT /about/employers/{position}/site {url}` (types a site; 400 `invalid_site`/`site_not_allowed`) · `POST /about/extra?locale {text}` (1 call; 1 to 4,000 characters, else 400 `invalid_text`) · `PUT|DELETE /about/changes/{id}` · `GET /about/review` · `POST /about/save`. No profile: 409 `profile_needed`. No model: 503 `ai_unavailable`. Over the cap: 429 `about_daily_cap` before any call. Budget and rate problems are the gateway's (`ai_budget_reached`, `ai_daily_limit_reached`, `ai_failed`), never retried. Logs carry counts and the prompt version only, never a sentence or an answer.

## AI use
Role `complete` (not `decide`). Without it: `ai_unavailable`, the page says so, and every other part of the app works. The person can undo anything before Save (a choice can be taken back, a change removed); after Save everything is an ordinary profile edit.

## Client
Route `/profile/about`, in the app frame. Entry: the strength card's primary button is "Tell me about you" (the Improve button is gone from the card; its route remains). Screen 1: the eyebrow and title, the lead ("I only ask you what I can't find out myself"), the legend, the portrait as flowing text whose assumptions are **buttons** (keyboard reachable) opening a panel with *I assumed*, *Why* and the three choices, the employers I looked at (add a site; update the portrait), *Is this accurate?* chips, the *Anything else* textarea, Continue and the cost line. While the portrait is made: "Reading your profile and your past employers' websites…". Then "Added from what you wrote" (changes with their words and flags, the updated portrait with the new sentences outlined, "Questions about the gaps come next."), then the review ("Here's you now") with Save and, after it, the new strength. Spans and panel come from a `Portrait` composition in `lib/ui`; tokens only. Four languages; no sideways scroll at 375px. The profile page shows `background` and labels a confirmed assumption under its role.
